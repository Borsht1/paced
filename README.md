# Paced

A to-do list that helps you work at a steady pace and balance work with free time.

- Add a task and split it into small steps, with a guess of how many minutes each will take.
- **Import**, in Settings, brings in tasks from elsewhere. **Paste a list** turns each line of any list into a task, and works everywhere. **Google Tasks** shows your open tasks with a checkbox beside each. Tick the ones you want and they become Paced tasks, with due dates kept and subtasks as steps. Paced only reads from Google, so finishing a task here doesn't check it off there. Tasks you've already imported are marked so you don't add them twice. Google Tasks works on the website only.
- **Next up**, at the top, names the one thing to start: what's first on your timeline, or else what's due soonest, or else the top of your list. One press of **Start** begins it.
- If yesterday's timeline left tasks unfinished, the top asks about them first. **Put on today** adds them to today's timeline, and **Not now** leaves them off.
- The main screen is a plain list, like Google Tasks. **Add a task** at the top makes a task from one line: type and press Enter. You can end it with minutes and a day, like `chem lab 40m fri`, and the line under the box shows what it understood. Tap a task afterwards for its details: a due date, a day and time to work on it, and steps. Each task is one row: a circle to tick it off, its name, one grey line (time left, steps, when it's planned, when it's due), and ▶ to start it. **Completed** sits at the bottom of the list.
- **Work on it**, beside Due date in a task's details or a new task, plans it for a day: today or any later one, with a start time if you like. On today it goes on the timeline, starting no sooner than that time. A later day keeps it until that day comes, and your list shows when it's planned.
- A task past its due date says so once, calmly, with **Move to today** beside it.
- Press **Start**. Paced counts down each step and stops you at the planned end to decide what's next. Once it has timed a few of your steps, the timer plans the time you really take, so if your steps usually run 20% over your guesses, a 40-minute guess gets 48 minutes, and the line under the timer says so.
- Past the planned end the clock counts up in amber, and turns red only well past it (a quarter of the plan, at least 5 minutes). Paced reminds you once at the end and once more a little later, both scaled to the step, and stops the timer by itself if nobody answers: 30 minutes past a short step, a quarter of the plan past a long one. More time comes in amounts that fit the step (5, 10 or 15 minutes, or 15, 30 or 60 for an hour or more), as often as you need.
- A task of quick steps (all under 10 minutes, or with no minutes) runs as a **checklist**: one timer for the whole list, and you tick steps off as you go, in any order, with no stop after each one. Time's up comes once, when the list's time runs out. **One timer for the whole list** in a task's details turns this on or off for any task with two or more steps.
- On a step of 45 minutes or more, the break asks once how far along you are (a quarter, half or three quarters). From that Paced works out how long the whole step will take at your pace and offers to plan that instead, so a long step doesn't surprise you at the end.
- Between steps a one-minute clock runs, then the next step starts by itself, so nothing is left un-timed. When a task is finished and nothing is planned after it, Paced waits for you to choose, and the clock is how long until it reminds you.
- The Today timeline sits above your list, folded to one line (what's planned and what's next) until you tap it. Tap **Today** again to fold it. Dragging a task from the list opens it so you can drop the task where you want. Each device remembers whether it's open. Open, it shows the hours you can work. It stays in view while you drag a task onto it. Drag the grip under it to make it taller or shorter. Scroll sideways to see the rest of the day, and **Back to now** returns. Drag tasks onto it and each takes up room for the time it needs. Tasks flow through your free time in order and never start sooner than where you dropped them, so changing a task, your work hours or running over moves everything after it, and anything that no longer fits turns red. Drag either end of your work hours, or use **Work hours** under **Options**. Your changes last for the day, unless you choose to use them every day. **Clear today**, also under **Options**, empties the day.
- On the timeline your tasks are in your color from Settings, Google Calendar events are gray and breaks are green. A task outlined instead of filled has steps with no minutes yet.
- Click a task on the timeline to start it, remove it from today or put a different task there. Click a break to change its length, move it, add another, start it now or delete it. Backspace or Delete removes what's selected, and you can undo it. Drag the right edge of a task, or use the minute buttons in its menu, to save more or less time for it today. Its steps and timers stay as they are.
- Drag across the timeline to select, so you can delete or change many at once. If you select both tasks and breaks, the menu lets you choose which to change. Drag along the hour labels to start anywhere, shift-click to add to what's selected, and drag across an empty stretch to block it off or add a break there.
- **Google Calendar** under **Options** shows today's events as fixed blocks on the timeline, and tasks flow around them. Pick which calendars to use the first time, and press it again to update. All-day events, ones marked Free and ones you declined are left out. Click an event to hide it for today. While Paced is open, it warns you 5 minutes before an event. Paced can't update by itself, so press **Update calendar** when it appears each day, and Google may ask you to approve it again. **Settings** shows what's connected and turns it off. This works on the website only.
- A task's steps are in its details. Starting a different task while one is running sets the running step aside with its time kept, and a finished step in the timer's Steps list has **Go back**, which starts it again.
- **Schedules** under **Options** saves today's hours, breaks and tasks under a name. Load one into today or any later day, and it's waiting when that day comes.
- Drag **Add break** onto the timeline to plan one, or press it to add one after your next task. Tasks flow around it, and a break you planned comes before any Paced would suggest. When a task ends, the next one on the timeline starts by itself, and Paced alerts you when the first thing on it is due.
- Coming back after 30 minutes or more away from a paused step, such as overnight, counts as your break, so Paced doesn't ask for another one.
- While something is running, the bar at the bottom has **Stop**, so you can stop working without opening the timer.
- It tells you when to take a break once you slow down, or to switch tasks when something is due soon.
- Sound, notifications and the tab title reach you when you're not looking at Paced. Notifications need the page open in a desktop browser; on most phones you get sound and vibration only.
- **Pop out timer** opens a small timer window that stays on top of your other apps, with the current step, the time left, and buttons to finish or pause. It turns red when you run over. It works in desktop Zen, Firefox and Chrome, opens only when you press the button, and closes with the Paced tab.
- When you stop using your computer, the step pauses from the moment you stopped and carries on when you're back. It's on by default, and you can change it in Settings. It only sees this window, so time in other apps counts. When a step ends or you press Stop after most of it was spent away from Paced, it asks once whether all of it was work, and you can say how long you really worked. "Still working?" check-ins are off unless you turn them on in Settings.
- Paced asks how a step went once a day, until it has timed enough steps to know your speed.
- Starting work never begins with a question. If you were already working before you opened Paced, **Already working before this?** in the timer's status panel adds that time for the first 15 minutes of your first session of the day, so your first break comes sooner.
- When you stop, Paced shows a summary of the session: what you worked on, your breaks and pauses, and how long each took, with room for a note. Each session is saved, breaks included.
- **Templates** keep a finished task to do again. In **Completed tasks**, press **Save as template**. Later, pick it under **Start from a template** in the New task sheet, press **Use** beside it in Completed tasks, or just type its name in quick add ("lab report fri"). Each step starts at the time it really took, the middle of its last 5 real times, instead of a guess, so Paced doesn't add your usual extra time on top. Finishing a task made from a template teaches the template its new times by itself. Steps that were never timed keep your guess.
- A template can **repeat**: press **Repeat** beside it in Completed tasks and pick one or more days of the week. On each of those days Paced adds the task by itself, due that day and on that day's timeline, starting from the next one after you pick. If Paced wasn't open on a repeat day, the task is added the next time you open it, but only the latest one, so a week away doesn't leave a pile. Tap a day again to stop it.
- **Your performance**, at the top of History, shows one day or one week at a time, with arrows to go back. A few sentences say what stands out, such as more focus than the day before, your quickest time of day, or a slowdown after an hour without a break. Four tiles show focus time, steps done, your usual pace and how many steps finished on time, each compared with the period before (for a day or week still going, with the same stretch of the one before). Two charts follow: focus time by hour or day, and your pace step by step through each session, with breaks shaded and 100% meaning as planned. Point at or tab to any column or dot for its details, or open **Show as a table**.
- History shows your past sessions and when you work best: by time of day, by day of the week, and by how long it's been since a break.
- Try different ways of working (Break when you slow down, Pomodoro, Long focus, Switch tasks) and see which suits you.
- **Notes** catch a thought while you work so you don't lose it. Type it in the box on the timer screen and press Enter, or press **N** anywhere in Paced, or use the **Notes** button at the top left. The timer keeps running. Each note remembers when you wrote it and what step you were on. **Make it a task** turns a note into a task, with any extra lines as its steps. The summary when you stop working lists the thoughts you jotted. Deleting a note offers **Undo**.

## Running it

The whole app is one file, `paced.html`. Open it in a browser and it works. Tasks are saved in that browser.

When it runs as a Claude artifact on claude.ai, tasks are saved to your Claude account instead.

## Website (GitHub Pages)

The site is at https://khaserlfuiaweo.github.io/paced/. `index.html` is the version for the website. After changing `paced.html`, rebuild it with:

```sh
sh build-pages.sh
```

## Accounts on the website

Sign-in and saving across devices use Firebase, on the website only.

- `firebase-config.js` holds the project's settings. Set it to `null` to run the site without accounts.
- `firestore.rules` holds the privacy rules. Each person can read and change only their own tasks.

## Google on the website

`PACED_GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` in `firebase-config.js` turns on Google Tasks and Google Calendar. While it's `null`, both are hidden.

The client ID comes from the `paced-5dbc5` project in the [Google Cloud console](https://console.cloud.google.com/). The project needs the Google Tasks API and Google Calendar API turned on, the `tasks.readonly` and `calendar.readonly` scopes on its sign-in screen, and a web client that allows `https://khaserlfuiaweo.github.io`. Firebase Authentication needs the same domain in its authorized domains.

While the sign-in screen is in Testing, only the people added as test users can connect, up to 100, and they see an "unverified app" warning. Removing the warning for everyone takes Google's verification, which can take months. Publishing without verification lets anyone connect, but only 100 people ever can, and that limit can't be reset.

## Testers

People testing Paced can leave notes in a shared **Tester notes** panel, with a tab for each tester and an **All** tab. Open it on the website with `?debug` at the end of the address (https://khaserlfuiaweo.github.io/paced/?debug), or press Ctrl+Shift+D (⌘⇧D on a Mac). Each note also saves what was on screen, the window and screen size, the browser, light or dark, the build and any recent errors. It saves nothing from your tasks, and no email. Testers can delete only their own notes. Anyone who isn't a tester sees nothing.

To set it up once, in the [Firebase console](https://console.firebase.google.com/) for `paced-5dbc5`:

1. **Firestore → Rules**: paste in `firestore.rules` from this folder and press **Publish**. Pushing to GitHub doesn't change the rules.
2. **Firestore → Data**: add a collection called `testers`. For each tester, add a document whose **document ID is the email they sign in with**, with a field `name` (text) for their tab. Add yourself too.

To remove a tester, delete their document. Their notes stay.
